\title{From Raw Hardware Manuals to Checkable Operation Contracts:\\
Provenance-Anchored Extraction, Evidence-Conditioned Plans,\\
and Refusable Completion Claims}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Double-blind submission}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language models are increasingly used to generate device drivers and
initialization sequences from chip manuals, yet existing evaluations mostly
operate on \emph{curated} specifications or reference implementations. They
avoid three properties of real documentation: requirements span
\emph{multiple files and sentences}; verification actions themselves have
\emph{side effects} (reading a shadowed register resets its write phase);
and when evidence is insufficient, the correct behavior is to \emph{refuse}
rather than to answer. We present an auditable pipeline. First, we extract
\emph{contract tables} from fixed-commit raw manuals---each entry anchored
by a verbatim quote, file, line number, and file hash (22 entries across
two IPs, 100\% machine-verified), with documented contradictions retained
explicitly rather than adjudicated. Second, contracts compile to
deterministic CPU checkers that replay operation sequences against
scenario oracles and emit four-way verdicts: success, violation (with
contract ID), insufficient evidence, or budget infeasible. Third, under
identical documents, budget, and a frozen protocol, we compare three
methods: direct generation, generation with per-step citations, and a
pipeline of structured extraction, compiled self-checking, and one
repair round. Across ten task variants on OpenTitan AES and SPI\_HOST:
direct generation succeeds on 2/10, cited generation on 1/10 (citations
verify against stated lines in 0/42 cases), and the pipeline on 9/10
while submitting zero plans with known violations. Structured extraction
recovers the complete contract on 9/10 variants---including reporting
the manuals' own contradiction as an entry. Ablations localize the
direct-generation failure to cross-sentence evidence obligations
(12/12 violations, reproduced by a larger model) and attribute the
pipeline's gains to two compiled mechanisms: evidence-depth checking
($k{+}1$ polls) and observation-value simulation. All contract tables,
checkers, frozen protocols, call ledgers, and raw outputs are open.
\end{abstract}

hardware manuals, operation contracts, provenance,

\section{Introduction}
Embedded software correctness hinges on a handful of cross-file manual
facts. After configuring the AES control register and before writing the
initial key, software must \emph{re-check} that the unit is idle---a
requirement assembled from two adjacent sentences in the programmer's
guide plus a semantics section of the shared register-tool documentation.
No single sentence states it completely. Similar structures govern
SPI\_HOST's ``exactly one more segment may queue'' boundary. LLM-based
driver generation is growing rapidly, but evaluations largely bypass such
\emph{conditional evidence obligations}: curated field lists, reference
RTL scoring, or treating a returned value as hardware completion.

\subsection{Gap}
Neighbor works each cover part of the space; three combined properties
remain untested simultaneously: (i) the input is \emph{raw multi-file
documentation} with real cross-references, redundancy, and
contradictions; (ii) \emph{side effects of verification actions} enter
the verdict---checking a shadowed register changes its state, so
``poll once more'' is not free; (iii) \emph{completion claims are
conditioned} on actually observed readiness, and refusal must be
distinguishable from violation. Driver verification (Termite-2~\cite{termite2})
assumes a device model is given; NL-to-formula work (nl2spec~\cite{nl2spec})
handles single sentences; embedded-agent benchmarks~\cite{embedbench}
use curated tasks; assertion generation~\cite{assertllm2} targets SVA.
We claim no ``first''; the contribution is the combination and its
auditability.

\subsection{This paper}
We recast manual-to-sequence generation as manual~$\rightarrow$~contract~%
$\rightarrow$~checker~$\rightarrow$~plan, and run controlled experiments
asking: what exactly do strong models lose when planning directly from
raw documents, and how much of the gap can machinery recover
mechanically?

Contributions, each mapped to an experiment:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Provenance-anchored contract extraction}
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:contract}): verbatim-quote/line/hash triple-anchored
      contract tables; documented contradictions retained (22 entries,
      100\% machine-verified).
\item \textbf{Contract-compiled checkers} (Sec.~\ref{sec:checker}):
      deterministic CPU state machines; four-way verdicts with replayable
      traces; 20 contract-level tests including shadow-reset and recovery
      paths.
\item \textbf{Controlled comparison and failure decomposition}
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}): ten paired variants, frozen protocols,
      per-call ledgers; cross-sentence obligation failures (12/12,
      model-general), unreliable citations (0/42), extraction
      completeness (9/10), interception/repair attribution, and two
      mechanism increments each validated by ablation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Driver verification and device models.} Termite-2~\cite{termite2}
synthesizes and verifies drivers over given device models, explicitly
distinguishing hardware events from software acknowledgments; its models
come from manual curation. We do not synthesize drivers; we test recovery
of checkable contracts from raw documents, with observation side effects
inside the same state machine.

\textbf{Runtime shielding and assumption learning.} Shield
synthesis~\cite{shield} minimally interferes given signal traces;
assumption learning~\cite{assume} infers environment assumptions for
given models. Our ``missing information $\rightarrow$ retained unknown''
echoes this, but evidence comes from document text, and learned
conditions are never promoted to manual promises.

\textbf{NL to specifications and assertions.} nl2spec~\cite{nl2spec}
maps single sentences to LTL fragments with interaction;
AssertLLM~\cite{assertllm} and AssertLLM2~\cite{assertllm2} generate SVA
from full specifications and score with FPV/mutation kill counts. Our
output is side-effectful MMIO operation sequences with conditioned
completion claims; our oracle needs no reference RTL.

\textbf{Embedded agents and repair benchmarks.}
EmbedBench~\cite{embedbench} evaluates agents with curated tasks on
simulators; DevGen~\cite{devgen} builds QEMU models from driver sources;
HWE-Bench~\cite{hwebench} does repository-level repair. Our input is
uncurated raw manuals, and we score evidence discipline, not task
completion.

\textbf{Evaluation integrity.} All batches were preregistration-frozen
before execution, with per-call ledgers; three infrastructure defects
(thinking-budget exhaustion, array-parse failure, accidental VCS sweep)
were declared void with full records retained---mirroring accepted
practice in measurement engineering.

\section{Contract Tables: Provenance-Anchored Extraction}
\label{sec:contract}

\subsection{Triple anchoring}
Each entry carries a verbatim quote, file, line number, and the file's
SHA-256 prefix; every entry is machine-verified to exist verbatim within
a $\pm2$-line window of the stated line. AES contributes 13 entries and
SPI\_HOST 9 (Tables in the artifact), covering preconditions (AES
C1/C4; SPI S1), ordering (C3; S3), protocols (C5 two-write; S2
queue exception), side effects (C6 read-resets-phase), error paths
(C7; S5), completion evidence (C8; S7), and an explicit cross-file
merge requirement (C10).

\subsection{Retained contradictions}
SPI's \texttt{EVENT\_ENABLE.IDLE} has two contradictory official
descriptions (guide line 145 vs.\ register table line 439). The conflict
is entry S8: both quotes recorded, \emph{not adjudicated}, and the
interrupt branch excluded from task scope. Notably, in extraction the
model itself emits a \texttt{CONFLICT\_RETAINED} entry in 3/4
variants---a structured interface invites reporting contradictions
rather than resolving them silently.

\subsection{No-guessing}
Unbounded busy windows (C13) and boundary cases are retained as unknowns;
checkers are parameterized accordingly (budget infeasibility is separated
from violation), never filled with defaults.

\section{Checkers: Contracts to Replayable Verdicts}
\label{sec:checker}
\texttt{AESInitChecker} and \texttt{SPISubmitChecker} compile contracts
into deterministic state machines: input is an operation sequence
(reads/writes with registers) plus a scenario object (a deterministic
read environment standing in for RTL); output is one of four
verdicts---\textsc{Success}, \textsc{Violation(contract id)},
\textsc{InsufficientEvidence}, \textsc{BudgetInfeasible}---with
per-step state snapshots. Two semantic rulings deserve note: a read
between the two shadowed writes resets the write phase (C6), so the
documented recovery path (write a fresh equal pair) is modeled; and
``transmission complete'' requires final observations of
\texttt{READY=1} \emph{and} \texttt{TXEMPTY=1} (S7)---readiness to
accept commands is not completion. Twenty contract-level tests pass,
including these subtle paths.

\section{Controlled Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
Task families: AES, six variants (busy windows 0/3/5/10; initially
busy; never ready); SPI, four variants (likewise parameterized).
Scenario parameters are visible to all methods. Information input is
byte-identical across methods (four to five full source files per IP,
fixed commit). Model: \texttt{glm-5.3-flash}, temperature 0, thinking
low; budgets frozen. Methods: (A) one direct call; (B) one call with
per-step citations; (C) extraction call + plan call + pre-submit
self-check + at most one checker-feedback repair. The oracle---checkers
compiled from our human contract tables---scores all methods and is
given to none (upper-bound principle). Fairness arm (v8): A and B
receive the same repair budget with feedback from a \emph{generic}
structural checker (existence-level rules only; no human contract, no
depth, no observation values). A second model (\texttt{glm-5.3},
larger) runs arm A unrepaired.

\subsection{Failure decomposition}
\textbf{(i) Cross-sentence obligations are a systematic blind spot.}
All 12 direct plans violate AES C1/C4---``re-check idle after
configuration'' is assembled from adjacent sentences plus shared
semantics; models read each sentence and lose the combination at
planning time. SPI's explicit single-sentence rules are far easier for
direct generation (A 2/4), giving the benchmark a measurable difficulty
spectrum by document structure.

\textbf{(ii) Citations do not fix planning, and are themselves
unreliable.} B's per-line citations verify against the stated lines in
0/42 cases (strict line-exact criterion)---fluent reference, wrong
referent.

\textbf{(iii) Extraction completeness is an interface effect.} The same
model under a structured extraction interface recovers the full contract
on 9/10 variants, including the conflict entry. Accessibility is not
the bottleneck; the interface is.

\textbf{(iv) Interception and repair, disentangled from budget.}
Same-budget generic repair lifts baselines to 4--5/10 by fixing
existence-level errors; the remaining gap (T2--T4, U2-class) maps
exactly onto the two compiled mechanisms: evidence depth---$k{+}1$
polls, because models proceed after $k$ busy readings without ever
observing readiness (the T2 lesson)---and observation-value simulation,
which rejects submitting a command after \emph{observing} not-ready
(U4). Both increments were validated by zero-API rescoring of saved
plans followed by single repair calls.

\textbf{(v) Honest refusal occurs across arms.} B$^+$ on the
never-ready SPI variant and the larger model on the never-ready AES
variant both stop with \textsc{InsufficientEvidence}---the four-way
verdict design is exercised by real behavior, not by fiat.

\subsection{Boundaries}
Ten variants across two families; one model family plus one
cross-check; the oracle is compiled from human (machine-adjudicated)
contract tables---weaker than independent dual adjudication, with all
materials open for re-auditing; formal-model-to-RTL consistency is
unverified (independent future work); C's repair feedback encodes
scenario compilation that baselines' generic feedback does not---this
is the mechanism under test, stated explicitly. No seed/temperature
repetition (owner decision).

\section{Discussion}
For the ``manuals as programs'' position, our evidence supports a
bounded strong claim: when correctness turns on cross-sentence evidence
obligations and observation side effects, letting models do
\emph{structured contract extraction} and deterministic checkers do
\emph{pre-submit adjudication} drives wrong submissions to zero and
multiplies end-to-end correctness on two real IPs. It does not support
``checkers necessarily raise final accuracy'' (the U4 counterexample
before value-awareness) nor ``extraction equals understanding''
(contradiction adjudication remains out of scope). For benchmark
builders, triple anchoring, retained contradictions, and refusal-grade
verdicts transfer directly to other IP families.

\section{Conclusion}
We recast manual-to-sequence generation as
manual$\rightarrow$contract$\rightarrow$checker$\rightarrow$plan, show
that strong models fail specifically at cross-sentence evidence
obligations, recover most of the gap with two attributable compiled
mechanisms, and localize what remains to observation-value semantics.
Data, code, protocols, and ledgers are open.
